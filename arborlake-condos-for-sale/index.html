About the Arborlake Community

Arborlake is a 190-unit condo community in southwest Carmel Valley, built by Baldwin Homes between 1986 and 1989. What sets it apart from newer 92130 developments is its water-feature setting: a central lake, multiple fountains and waterfalls, and paved walking paths winding through greenbelts that have had nearly four decades to mature.

Units range from 742-square-foot one-bedrooms to 1,145-square-foot two-bedroom layouts, with most patios and balconies oriented toward the lake and landscaping rather than the parking areas. The community sits just north of Carmel View Road and Camino Mira Del Mar — one of the earliest-developed pockets of Carmel Valley, so the surrounding sidewalks, parks, and street trees are as established as the grounds themselves.

As a Keller Williams Realty agent who has worked this community for 11+ years, I can walk you through current HOA details, recent comparable sales, and what to look for in a specific building or floor plan. Before You Buy

What to Know Before Buying at Arborlake

The honest tradeoffs, not just the highlights — so there are no surprises at inspection.

Show Details +Hide Details –

Carport parking, not a garage

Covered carport parking with storage is included, but it's not an enclosed garage — worth factoring in if that matters for your lifestyle or a second vehicle.

Floor plans vary by building

Some units are ground-floor walk-ups, others are upper-level with stairs — confirm the specific floor, elevator access, and layout before falling in love with a listing photo.

HOA covers the exterior, not the interior

The ~$408/mo HOA handles building exteriors, the lake, landscaping, and amenities. Interior updates — kitchens, baths, flooring — are the owner's responsibility, so ask about a specific unit's renovation history.

No private yard

Like any condo community, outdoor space is a patio or balcony, not a yard. If private outdoor space is a priority, compare unit size and patio orientation carefully — lake-facing units are the most sought after.

Market Analysis

Five Years of Arborlake Home Sales: What the Data Actually Shows

Arborlake is a small community — 190 units, a handful of closings a year — which means its sales data rewards a closer read than a headline number ever could.

The Big Picture: 22 Sales, Six Years

Between late 2021 and today, 22 Arborlake condos have closed escrow. That's not a large sample — some years saw as few as one or two sales, so 2021 and 2024 in particular are single data points, not statistically reliable averages — so the year-by-year numbers above are best read as a trend, not a guarantee. Still, the trend is legible: after a dip through 2022–2023, median prices have climbed in each of the last two full years.

Price Per Square Foot Is the Steadier Signal

Median price swings around in a market this small — a single larger or smaller unit selling in a given year can shift the headline number by tens of thousands of dollars. Price per square foot is a cleaner read, because it adjusts for exactly that. And on that measure, Arborlake's story is less bumpy than the median-price table suggests: a climb from $663/sq ft in 2021 to the $770s through 2022–2023, a dip in 2024, and then a clear move upward to $815/sq ft in 2025 and $833/sq ft so far in 2026.

Two consecutive years of rising price per square foot, even while the raw median bounced around, is the more trustworthy signal that Arborlake values are genuinely appreciating — not just that bigger units happened to sell.

A Tale of Two Unit Types

Arborlake's 1-bedroom and 2-bedroom units have moved differently enough over the last five years that lumping them into one number hides more than it reveals.

Two-bedroom units have been the steadier performer: 16 closings, a tight-ish range, and a trend line that points up across the five-year window. One-bedrooms are a different story — only six sales, a wider spread relative to their price point, and a flatter-to-slightly-declining trend. Some of that gap is a sample-size artifact (six sales is not much to draw a line through), but some of it likely reflects genuine demand: two-bedroom units suit a broader range of buyers — downsizers who still want a guest room, small families, investors targeting a wider renter pool — while one-bedrooms draw a narrower, more price-sensitive buyer. The one outlier worth noting: a 1-bedroom sold for $815,000 in the dataset, well above the 1-bed median, almost certainly reflecting an upgraded or larger-than-typical unit rather than a shift in the baseline 1-bed market.

Days on Market Tell Their Own Story

If price is what a home sold for, days on market is how hard the seller had to work for it. Arborlake's pattern here is noisy — again, small numbers — but the direction lines up with the price data: 2025's four sales averaged just 6 days on market, among the fastest of the five-year window, while 2024's two sales (one of which sat 79 days) averaged 62. That's consistent with a market that cooled into 2023–2024 and has since tightened back up, rewarding well-priced, well-presented units with quick sales again.

How the HOA Fits Into the Value Picture

None of this happens in a vacuum. Arborlake's HOA runs around $408/month — the highest of the nearby comparable communities this page tracks — and that premium buys something those comparables don't have: a maintained central lake with fountains and waterfalls, two tennis courts, a heated pool, two spas, a sauna, and a fitness center. The price and $/sq ft trends above suggest the market has been willing to pay for that, not penalize it — appreciation has continued even as the HOA gap versus nearby communities has stayed roughly constant.

What This Means If You're Buying or Selling

If you're buying: don't anchor too hard to any single closed sale — with this few transactions a year, one unusually large or small unit can swing the "median" meaningfully. Price per square foot and the direction of days-on-market are more reliable signals than any one closing. And know that 2-bedroom units have a longer, steadier sales history to lean on than 1-bedrooms do.

If you're selling: the data says pricing discipline matters more now than it did in the slower 2023–2024 stretch. The fastest, highest sales in this dataset — several under 10 days on market in 2025 — went to units priced right and presented well from day one, not to units that started high and chased the market down.

Good to Know

Arborlake Condo FAQs

What is the price range for Arborlake condos?

Arborlake condos typically sell between $650,000 and $875,000, or roughly $680–$820 per square foot depending on the unit's condition and position within the community.

What do Arborlake HOA fees cover?

Arborlake's HOA runs approximately $408 per month and covers the community's central lake and fountain system, two lighted tennis courts, a heated pool, two spas, a dry sauna, a fitness center, all greenbelt landscaping, exterior building maintenance, and covered carport parking with storage.

Does Arborlake have Mello-Roos?

No — Arborlake carries no Mello-Roos assessment, with a property tax rate of approximately 1.07% of assessed value.

How big are the units at Arborlake?

Arborlake units range from 742-square-foot one-bedrooms to 1,145-square-foot two-bedroom layouts.

How does Arborlake compare to other Carmel Valley condo communities?

Arborlake's HOA runs higher than comparable communities like The Lakes (~$397/mo), Carmel Pointe (~$350/mo), and Del Mar Villas (~$340/mo) — the difference mostly funds the central lake, fountains, and waterfalls that those communities don't have.

How quickly do Arborlake condos sell?

Well-priced, updated listings typically go under contract within 2–3 weeks, reflecting a buyer pool that often targets this specific community rather than comparison-shopping broadly.
